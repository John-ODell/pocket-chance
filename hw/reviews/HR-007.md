# HR-007: Review of DR-007, how the bankroll is saved to flash

- **Verdict: fits**
- Reviewer: microcontroller expert, 2026-10-03. Numbers from `hwtest/flash_write.py` (wrote and removed `/_hwtest_tmp.json` and `/_hwtest_tmp.new` only).

## Numbers
| Operation (41-byte JSON) | Mean | Min | Max |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plain overwrite of the file | 2.9 ms | 2.5 ms | 3.3 ms |
| Write to tmp then `os.rename` over the target (atomic) | **22 ms** | 6 ms | **79 ms** |

- Once per finished round, 22 ms (occasionally 79 ms) is invisible if done between hands. It would be a visible hitch inside an animation, so save when the screen is static.
- Endurance: the class figure for this type of NOR flash is ~100,000 erase cycles per 4 KB block (datasheet-class, not measured), and LittleFS moves writes across free blocks, of which there are ~340. A round every 5 seconds for 10 hours a day is 7,200 saves a day; the dev's "once per round" is safe for the life of the device. Saving on every button press or every frame would not be, and is the one thing to forbid.
- `os.rename` over an existing file is atomic on LittleFS: after power loss either the old or the new `save.json` exists. The 79 ms worst case is LittleFS housekeeping (block relocation), not a fault.

## What the dev must stay inside
1. Save **once per round**, after the payout, before the next deal, never in the draw loop.
2. Always tmp + rename. The 20 ms extra over a plain write buys the power-cut guarantee.
3. Call `gc.collect()` is not needed before a save; `json.dumps` of three fields is a small allocation.
4. Expect up to ~80 ms and do not start an animation until the save returns.

## Not measured
Behaviour on an actual power cut mid-write. I will not pull power on the board without John present; LittleFS's design covers it.
