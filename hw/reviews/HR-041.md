# HR-041: Review of DR-041, Caribbean Stud AI seat rail

- **Verdict: fits.** Every number the request claims is met or beaten.
- Reviewer: microcontroller expert, 2026-10-04. Script `hwtest/stud_rail_bench.py`: five seats dealt five real cards each from a shuffled deck, each deciding with the board's `stud_rules.advice()` after `poker.evaluate()` on its hand against the dealer's up-card; the 12-row rail drawn as the request describes (five stacks of up to six 2 px lines, a 4x4 green/red marker) and pushed; real `lib/` (step 1j) on the board.

## Reading
- Five seats cost about 10 ms of engine time per hand in total (decide + settle), inside the pauses the game already has. Nothing to schedule around.
- The rail is cheap enough to redraw with every bottom-band update; no separate band push is needed.
- Five more hands from one deck is the dev's point about odds, not mine; hardware does not care.

## Limits for the dev
1. Draw the rail as part of the bottom band's normal redraw (it adds under 2 ms); do not give it its own push unless it changes alone.
2. Run the seats' decisions after the player's raise/fold and before the reveal starts, in the same place the dealer's hand is settled; 10 ms there is invisible.
3. Keep the rail's text-free design; size-1 text for names would not fit 12 px anyway.
