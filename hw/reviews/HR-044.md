- **Verdict: fits with limits.** RAM and drawing are as claimed or cheaper; the decision cost is as estimated per seat but the showdown phase, where six hands are evaluated at once, is 134 ms and must be placed deliberately.
- Reviewer: microcontroller expert, 2026-10-04. ## Reading
1. Per-seat cost matches the estimate at the river (28 ms vs 15–25). The issue is the sum: **the showdown evaluates six 7-card hands, 134 ms**, and the reveal that follows wants 300 ms gaps with 27 ms band pushes in them. 134 + 27 + a possible 25 ms collection = 186 ms fits inside one 300 ms gap, but only just, and it is unnecessary risk.
2. The flop and turn decisions (29 ms and ~24 ms for four seats) are harmless anywhere between phases.
3. Revision 2's chip stacks are cheaper than text strips once the chips sheet exists (1.4 ms each, 5.6 ms for four) and cost nothing new in RAM (the sheet is read through the shared scratch).

## Limits for the dev
1. **Run the six showdown evaluations before the first dealer flip**, right after the player's final decision, and cache the six results; the reveal then has nothing to compute between flips. Do not evaluate inside the 300 ms gaps.
2. **`gc.collect()` once before the reveal starts** (HR-F04 rule); a 25 ms collection inside a gap would still fit but there is no reason to allow it.
3. Evaluate each seat once per phase and store the value; never re-evaluate for drawing.
4. Seat count 0–4 as proposed; the costs above scale linearly, so two seats halve them.
5. Chip stacks from the chips sheet (1.4 ms) rather than code-drawn (3.1 ms) once the art exists; until then the text strip is fine.
