# HR-005: Review of DR-005, how assets are loaded from flash and drawn

- **Verdict: fits with limits**
- Reviewer: microcontroller expert, 2026-10-03. Numbers from `hwtest/flash_read.py`, `hwtest/frame_full.py`, `hwtest/frame_partial.py`, `hwtest/ram_free.py`.

## Numbers (replacing the dev's estimates)
| Dev's estimate | Measured |
|---|---|
| "a few KB from flash should be about a millisecond" | 4,480-byte card: **~1 ms** (4.4–5.6 MB/s). Correct. |
| "re-reading the table each frame costs tens of milliseconds" | 115,200-byte background: **20.5 ms** (one 32 KB-chunk read) to **25.9 ms** (480-byte row chunks). Correct. |
| full-frame redraw | `show()` alone is **18.5 ms**. Background read + 6 cards + chips + text + show ≈ **45–50 ms ≈ 20 fps** (sprite blit time is an estimate; the 48x48 scratch+blit path measured 3.9 ms including the push). |
| peak extra RAM about 16 KB | fits: **104 KB free after framebuffer, largest block 84 KB**. A 16 KB scratch leaves 87.6 KB. |

## Limits
1. **Do not re-read the table every frame.** It is a quarter of a frame budget on its own. Read it on scene entry and when something was drawn over it. Between rounds nothing moves, so a blackjack screen will mostly be redrawn only on input. The dev already offers this; make it the plan, not the fallback.
2. **Preallocate the 16 KB scratch once** and reuse it for every sprite (wrap it in a `FrameBuffer` of the sprite's own size each time: `FrameBuffer(memoryview(scratch)[:w*h*2], w, h, RGB565)` costs no new buffer). Allocating per sprite will fragment the heap and eventually fail (largest block already 84 KB of 104 KB).
3. **Partial pushes are much cheaper than `show()`.** A 240-wide band of 48 rows pushes in 7.2 ms; a 48x48 window in 3.9 ms. When only one card or the chip count changes, redraw that region in the framebuffer and push its band. Full `show()` on scene change only.
4. **Read in big chunks.** `readinto` the whole sprite in one call; for the background, one call into the framebuffer (that is the 20.5 ms figure). Row-by-row reading costs 25% more.
5. The transparent-key `blit` is a per-pixel compare in C. Fine for cards; measured indirectly inside the 3.9 ms figure. Not measured in isolation.

## Recommendation
Option A as the dev proposes, with limits 1–4 as part of the design, not as later optimisation. Option B (cache a few sprites) is not needed at these flash speeds; a card is 1 ms. Option C is not needed either.

## Not measured
Blit-with-key speed in isolation; real RAM after the dev's modules load. Both will be added to `BUDGET.md` after the first upload.
